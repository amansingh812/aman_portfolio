# Blog content standard — images, length, structure

Written 25 Sep 2026. Applies to every post in `content/blog.js` / `blog-content.js`,
guides and landing-page articles.

---

## 1. Length — write to the intent, not a word count

Google, verbatim, from its helpful-content guide, listed as a **warning sign**:
> "Are you writing to a particular word count because you've heard or read that
> Google has a preferred word count? (No, we don't.)"

Length correlates with ranking only because thorough answers tend to be long.
Padding to hit 2,000 words makes a page worse, not better.

**Working targets (a range to check coverage, not a quota):**

| Post type | Natural length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cost / pricing pillar | 2,000–3,000 | Many sub-questions: tiers, hosting, ongoing, hidden costs |
| Comparison (X vs Y, best agencies) | 1,800–2,800 | Criteria, table, verdict per use case |
| Checklist / how-to | 1,000–1,800 | People want the list, not an essay |
| Industry (NDIS, tradie) | 1,500–2,500 | Rules + costs + examples |

**The real test:** open the top 3 results for the keyword. List every
sub-question they answer. Our post must answer all of them **plus at least one
thing none of them has** (our own numbers, a real build, a worked quote).

Current state (25 Sep): posts run 1,080–1,987 words. The short ones to deepen
first are the ones with impressions in GSC, not the shortest ones.

## 2. What every post should contain

In this order:

1. **Answer first.** 2–3 sentences under the H1 that answer the query outright
   ("A small business website in Australia costs $800–$5,000…"). This is what
   featured snippets and AI Overviews lift.
2. **Key takeaways box**: 3–5 bullets.
3. **One original data point**, such as our published prices, a real (anonymised)
   quote breakdown, or a PageSpeed result from a real build. This is the part
   competitors cannot copy.
4. **A comparison or decision table.** At least one per post.
5. **First-hand experience**, e.g. "When we rebuilt X we found…". It must be
   real, and you need client permission to name them (§1 of CLAUDE.md).
6. **"Who this isn't for"**: an honest paragraph. It builds trust and people
   rarely write it.
7. **FAQ** (4–8): pulled from GSC queries and People Also Ask, not invented.
8. **Sources + "verified on" date** for any third-party price.
9. **≥3 internal links out**, with descriptive anchors, to pricing, the service
   page and one related post.
10. **Dual CTA**: quote + free call.

The byline (`By Aman Singh · date · read time`) is **required**. Google:
"Do pages carry a byline, where one might be expected?" Set `updated:` in
`blog.js` only after a substantive edit; bumping dates without real changes is
also on Google's warning list.

## 3. Images

### Where images go in a post

| Spot | Image type |
|---|---|
| Top (already done) | Hero, generated by `scripts/generate-blog-images.sh` |
| At each price table | Bar/range chart of the same numbers (SVG) |
| Process / timeline section | Step diagram |
| "What you get" section | Real screenshot of a live client build |
| Speed / performance claims | Real PageSpeed Insights screenshot |
| Comparison section | Side-by-side screenshot or feature grid |

Aim for **one useful image per ~300–400 words**. Every image must explain
something; decoration-only images add weight and nothing else.

The renderer already supports it. Add a block to `blog-content.js`:
```js
{ type: "image", src: "/assets/imgs/blog/<slug>/<name>.webp",
  alt: "Bar chart of Australian website costs from $800 to $5,000 by package",
  caption: "Build First Site package prices, verified 25 Sep 2026",
  width: 1200, height: 675 }
```

### Sources, best first

**1. Original images (best for ranking and trust).** Google's image guidance
favours original images, and stock photos appear on thousands of sites.
- Screenshots of real client sites: hsracegear.com, mobilearmour.com.au,
  autozenlyai.com, planet.ltfinance.com
- PageSpeed / Lighthouse / GSC screenshots
- Charts and diagrams built from our own data (Claude can generate these as SVG)
- Concept builds are fine **if captioned "concept build"**

**2. AI-generated (for concepts and illustrations).**
- **Adobe Firefly**: trained on licensed content; Adobe offers IP
  indemnification on qualifying plans (Firefly's own models only, not partner models)
- Our existing pipeline: `ai-cli` via `scripts/generate-blog-images.sh`
- Others: GPT Image (ChatGPT), Google Imagen/Gemini, Ideogram (good for text
  in images), Midjourney
- **Never** AI-generate: people presented as clients or staff, fake
  screenshots, fake dashboards with invented metrics, or anything with a real
  brand's logo. That is the §1 fabrication problem.

**3. Free stock.**
- **Unsplash**: free commercial use, no attribution required, no indemnity
- **Pexels**: free commercial use
- **Pixabay**: free commercial use
- Caution: none of them guarantee model releases. Don't use a recognisable
  person in a way that implies they endorse us or are a client.

**4. Paid stock (when you need people, offices, or legal cover).**
- **Unsplash+**: per-file indemnification
- **Adobe Stock**, **Shutterstock**, **iStock/Getty**, **Depositphotos**
- Check current pricing at purchase; plans change often.

### Rules for every image

- **Download and self-host** in `public/assets/imgs/blog/<slug>/`. Never
  hotlink another site's image: it breaks when they move it, it's slower,
  and hotlinking without a licence is copyright infringement.
- Convert to **WebP**, ~1200px wide, under 150 KB.
- **Filename** describes it: `website-cost-australia-price-chart.webp`, not `img1.webp`.
- **Alt text** describes what's in it, including the keyword only if it's
  actually true of the image.
- **Caption** where useful. Google reads nearby text as image context.
- Keep a licence record: source URL + licence + date in the image's
  commit message or `docs/IMAGE-LICENCES.md`.

### Known image debt

- `/blog/` index cards still use Agon template art (`blog/2/img-*.png`,
  `img-news-1.png`) instead of each post's own hero. Swap them for `post.image`.
- Stock avatars removed 25 Sep 2026 (they showed a stranger's face captioned
  "Aman Singh").
