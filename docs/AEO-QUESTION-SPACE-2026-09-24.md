24 Sep 2026. Built from the Search Console export (572 queries), not from a
keyword tool. These are questions **real people actually typed** and saw us for.

## The finding that should change what we build next

| Query group | Queries | Median position |
|---|---|---|
| **Hosting / ownership / ongoing cost** | **47** | **42.0** |
| Everything else on the site | 525 | 83.0 |

**A 41-place gap.** The hosting-and-ownership cluster is far and away our
strongest topic, and it is not close.

Why: `/how-much-does-a-website-cost-australia/` carries one section —
*"Website hosting costs in Australia: $0 – $100/month"* — with a table of real
numbers (Vercel $0, Cloudflare $0, Siteground $8–25, Wix $20–50, Shopify
$39–399, AWS $50–200+). It is the only place on the site that answers *"what
does hosting actually cost"* with figures.

**One section of one page is outranking entire pages we built on purpose.**

That is the whole AEO lesson in one data point. Language models and AI Overviews
extract specific, tabular, checkable facts. Our competitors do not write this
section because "what will this cost me to run for five years" is not a sales
question — they are selling builds. It is, however, exactly what someone asks an
assistant.

## The counterintuitive one: "Australia" is hurting us

| | Queries | Median position |
|---|---|---|
| AU-qualified (australia, sydney, melbourne…) | 192 | **95.6** |
| Generic | 380 | **68.8** |

We rank **27 places worse** on Australia-qualified queries than generic ones.

That is the opposite of what the strategy assumed. The reading: the AU-qualified
space is contested by established local agencies with real link profiles
(Wolf IQ, WP Creative, Digital Nomads HQ), while the generic question space is
won on specificity — where we compete.

Note `website hosting cost australia` sits at **39**. So it is not that
"Australia" is poison; it is that the *topic* decides, and on our strong topic
the AU qualifier is fine.

**What follows:** stop assuming "+ Australia" in a title helps. Australian
buyers type generic questions too. Test the generic phrasing.

## What I would build

### 1. `/website-hosting-cost-australia/` — the clearest opportunity on the site

47 queries, median position 42, off the back of **one section** of another page.
Give it a page and it has a genuine shot.

It must answer, in tables with real numbers:
- What hosting costs per month, by platform
- What it costs per year
- Whether you can host free, and when you cannot
- What happens if you stop paying
- What "owning your website" actually means in practice

This is also the page that carries your sharpest argument — the five-year number
and code ownership — into the query space where you already rank best.

### 2. A verbatim Q&A layer

**147 of 572 queries (25%) are questions.** Answer them in the exact words
people used. An inserted word breaks the match.

Grouped by theme, with impressions:

| Theme | Queries | Impressions | Best position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost / price | 121 | 330 | 32 |
| Time / how long | 22 | 59 | 36 |
| Hosting / ongoing | 11 | 24 | 36 |
| Ownership | 8 | 10 | 37 |
| Hiring / who | 2 | 7 | 62 |
| DIY / platform | 1 | 3 | 71 |

The shape to write in, every time: **answer in the first sentence, then
elaborate.** Models lift the first clean sentence; so do featured snippets.

### 3. What NOT to do

- **No separate "AEO/GEO optimisation".** Google's own guidance says generative
  AI features are rooted in core Search ranking. There is no trick layer.
- **Do not chase the AU-qualified cost head terms harder.** Median 95.6 against
  entrenched competition. The generic question space is winnable now.
- **Do not add llms.txt-style files expecting visibility.** Covered in
  CLAUDE.md §1 — no major provider uses them.
