## TUESDAY

### Post 1 — 7:30am (HOT TAKE — THREAD)
🧵 Why I stopped recommending WordPress for new business sites in 2026 (1/6)

1/6 Not a hot take for the sake of it — I built on WordPress for years. Here's what changed.

2/6 Plugin bloat is still the #1 cause of slow sites I audit. 40+ plugins, three page builders stacked on top of each other, and nobody remembers why half of them are active.

3/6 Security patching never stops. A WordPress site is a target the day it launches. Modern frameworks like Next.js ship far less attack surface by default.

4/6 "Easy to edit yourself" often means "easy to break yourself." I've fixed more broken WordPress sites from a bad plugin update than from any hack.

5/6 Modern stacks (Next.js, React, serverless hosting) load faster, rank better on Core Web Vitals, and don't need a monthly plugin-update ritual to stay secure.

6/6 WordPress isn't dead. It's just not what I'd build a growth-focused business site on in 2026. Full comparison on the site if you want the detail: buildfirstsite.com
Hashtags: #NextJS #WordPress #WebDev

## THURSDAY

### Post 1 — 7:30am (HOT TAKE — THREAD)
🧵 The real cost of a "free" website builder (1/5)

1/5 Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy Builder — the monthly fee looks cheap. $16-40/month. Do the math over 3 years and you're at $1,000+ with zero code ownership at the end.

2/5 You also can't take that site anywhere else. Cancel the subscription, lose the site. There's no export-and-move option that keeps your design intact.

3/5 SEO on these platforms is capped by design — limited schema control, bloated JS, slow mobile load times baked into the templates.

4/5 Compare that to a $2,000 one-time build on a modern stack: you own the code, host it anywhere, and it's built for speed and SEO from day one.

5/5 "Free" builders aren't free. They're a slow-drip subscription for a site you never actually own. Full cost breakdown: buildfirstsite.com
