How Long Does It Take
to Build a Website?

The honest answer: 2–3 weeks for most business websites, 4–6 weeks for e-commerce, and up to 3 months for complex web apps. Here's what those timelines actually look like — and what makes projects run long.

By Aman Singh, Build First Site · Last updated July 2026

Timeline by website type

01

1–2 weeks

Landing Page

A landing page is a single-page website. It covers one topic — a service, a product, a promotion — and drives visitors to one action (call, book, buy). It's the fastest type of professional website to build.

Typical 2-week timeline:

Days 1–2Brief, scope, design direction agreed
Days 3–7Design and build in Next.js + Tailwind
Days 8–10Client review on staging link, revisions
Days 11–14Final checks, domain setup, launch

Best for: tradies, service businesses, Google Ads campaigns, product launches, event pages. Tradie landing pages from $800 AUD →

02

2–3 weeks

Business Website (Multi-Page)

The most common type of website for established businesses. Usually 5–10 pages: Home, About, Services (sometimes one page per service), Portfolio/Gallery, and Contact. Built for SEO and long-term growth.

Typical 3-week timeline:

Week 1Discovery, sitemap agreed, content collected from client, design started
Week 2Full build in Next.js — all pages coded, mobile-tested, SEO setup
Week 3Client reviews staging site, two rounds of revisions, domain connected, launch

Best for: restaurants, real estate agents, tradies with multiple services, professional firms, small agencies. Business websites from $1,900 AUD →

03

4–6 weeks

E-Commerce Store

An online store has many moving parts: product catalogue, cart, checkout, payment processing, order confirmation emails, inventory tracking, and usually an admin dashboard. 4–6 weeks is realistic for a clean, functional e-commerce build.

What adds time in e-commerce:

→Number of products (10 vs 500 is a big difference)
→Variants (sizes, colours, custom options)
→Shipping rules (flat rate vs weight-based vs location-based)
→Product photos — must be provided before build can finish
→Third-party integrations (Xero, shipping labels, reviews apps)

Best for: retail brands, fashion, motorsport, specialty goods, anything you sell online. E-commerce from $4,500 AUD →

04

6–12 weeks

Web Application / SaaS

Web apps — SaaS products, AI tools, booking systems, dashboards, member portals — are significantly more complex. They involve user authentication, databases, APIs, and custom logic. 6 weeks is the minimum for a simple app; complex products can take 3–6 months.

Rough app timeline:

Weeks 1–2Product scoping, database design, architecture decisions
Weeks 3–5Core feature build (auth, data model, main workflows)
Weeks 6–8Front-end polish, mobile, edge case handling
Weeks 9–12Testing, Stripe billing setup, deployment, soft launch

Best for: SaaS products, AI-powered tools, booking platforms, internal tools, marketplaces. AI web apps from $5,000 AUD →

What actually delays a website build

In my experience, the website build itself rarely causes delays. Almost every overrun comes from one of these five things.

📝

Waiting for content

The #1 cause of delays. A website can't launch without text, photos, and a logo. The client provides these, not the developer. If you haven't written your "About" page or taken photos of your work, the build stalls. Tip: gather all your content before you sign a proposal.

🔄

Scope changes mid-build

"Can we also add a booking system?" mid-build adds weeks. This is why we lock scope in a written proposal before starting. Changes after sign-off are quoted separately and extend the timeline.

⏳

Slow client feedback

We send a staging link. If it takes 2 weeks to review, the project is 2 weeks longer. We ask clients to give feedback within 3 business days — and we build that expectation into the proposal timeline.

🔗

Third-party integrations

Connecting to your CRM, booking system, or inventory software adds time. Sometimes the third-party API is slow to approve, or their documentation is unclear. We flag all integrations upfront and add buffer time to account for this.

🔑

Domain and DNS access

Connecting your domain is the final step — but if you've forgotten your registrar login or your domain is still with an old web developer, this can delay launch by days. Locate your domain access early.

How to get your website built faster

Three things you can do before signing a proposal that will cut your build time by 1–2 weeks.

01

Write your content first

Services, about, contact details, any testimonials you have. It doesn't have to be perfect — we refine it. But having a first draft means we can start building immediately after sign-off.

02

Get your photos ready

For a tradie: photos of your work. For a restaurant: food photos. For a service business: a headshot and any relevant visuals. Good phone photos are fine. This alone removes the #1 source of delays.

03

Know your domain login

Log into your domain registrar (GoDaddy, Cloudflare, Namecheap, etc.) before we start. If you've lost access, get it back now — not the day before launch.

FAQ

Common questions about website timelines

How quickly can you build a website if I need it urgently?

If you have all your content ready (text, photos, logo), we can build a landing page in 1 week. For a business website, 2 weeks is the minimum realistic timeline. Rush delivery for firm deadlines is possible with a surcharge — contact us to discuss your specific situation.

How long do large agency websites take to build?

Large marketing agency projects with many stakeholders, approval processes, and complex CMS integrations often take 3–6 months. That's not typical for small and medium business websites. Most of our clients are live within 4 weeks from sign-off.

What's included in the 2–3 week business website timeline?

Discovery and briefing, design, full build in Next.js, mobile optimisation, SEO setup (meta tags, schema markup, sitemap, Google Search Console submission), two rounds of revisions, domain connection, and a 30-day support window after launch.

Does the website timeline start from when I pay?

Yes — the build clock starts when we receive the 50% deposit and your content brief. The faster you provide your content, the faster we can deliver. Clients who have all content ready on day 1 consistently get their sites 3–5 days earlier than those who gather content as we go.
