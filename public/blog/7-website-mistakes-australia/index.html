7 Website Mistakes Australian Small Businesses Make
(And How to Fix Them)

Most Australian small business websites have at least three of these problems. Some have all seven. The good news: none of them require a full rebuild to fix — just knowing where to look.

By Aman Singh, Build First Site · July 2026 · 8 min read

I've reviewed hundreds of Australian small business websites over the past few years. Cafes, tradies, real estate agents, consultants, retailers — and the same mistakes come up again and again. The frustrating thing is that most of them are invisible to the business owner. The site looks fine. But to a potential customer — or to Google — it's quietly losing you money every day.

Here are the seven I see most often, ordered from most damaging to least. Check each one against your own site today.

01

Your site loads too slowly on mobile

This is the single most damaging problem a website can have in 2026. Over 70% of Australians browse on their phones. If your site takes more than 3 seconds to load, more than half of those visitors leave — before seeing a single word you've written.

Wix sites average a Lighthouse mobile score of 40–65. WordPress sites with too many plugins often land in the same range. Google has confirmed that page speed is a ranking factor — so a slow site doesn't just lose visitors, it ranks lower than faster competitors too.

How to check

Go to web.dev/measure and enter your URL. Look at your mobile score. Under 70 = problem. Under 50 = serious problem.

The fix

Compress every image before uploading (use Squoosh.app — free). Remove plugins or apps you don't use. If you're on Wix or WordPress and scoring below 60, the platform itself may be the constraint — a custom-built site on Vercel typically scores 90–100 out of the box.

02

Your phone number isn't tap-to-call on mobile

This one costs tradies, restaurants, and service businesses the most. A customer on their phone, ready to call — and your phone number is displayed as text they have to manually copy and dial. Or worse, it's only in the footer, below the fold, invisible until they scroll.

The friction of manually dialling a number is enough for most people to tap back and try a competitor who has a proper call button. In a world where Google search shows your competitor's number with one tap, your website needs to match that experience.

How to check

Open your website on your phone. Without scrolling, can you see your phone number? Can you tap it to call? If no to either — fix this today.

The fix

Add a sticky header or floating button on mobile with your number as an href="tel:+61..." link. On Wix/Squarespace, you can add a button element to your header linked to your phone number. On a custom site, your developer can add a fixed mobile CTA in one afternoon.

03

No Google reviews on your website

Australian consumers are sceptical. Before booking a tradie, trying a new restaurant, or hiring a consultant, most people check reviews. If your Google reviews aren't visible on your own website, you're missing your single most powerful trust signal.

It's not enough to have reviews on Google Business Profile. Many people who land directly on your website never think to search for your reviews separately. If you've earned those 4.8 stars, show them where people are already looking.

How to check

Open your homepage. Scroll through it. Do you see any customer reviews or star ratings? If not — add them.

The fix

Copy your best 3–4 Google reviews and add them as a testimonials section on your homepage. You can also embed a live Google review widget (Elfsight is popular for Wix/WordPress — about $9 AUD/mo). On a custom site, we build a review section using the Google Places API so reviews update automatically.

04

Your menu is a PDF (restaurants and cafes)

This one is specific to hospitality — and it's almost universal. Someone searches "pasta restaurant Fitzroy" on their phone at 7pm. They click your site. They tap "Menu". A PDF starts downloading. On mobile. In a dark restaurant. They close it and go to your competitor whose menu is a clean webpage.

PDFs also have zero SEO value. Google can't index your dishes, your dietary information, your specials. A webpage menu with your dish names, descriptions, and prices can rank for "[suburb] pasta" or "[suburb] vegan options" searches — a PDF cannot.

How to check

Open your menu link on your phone. Does it load a webpage or try to open a file? If it's a PDF or a photo, fix it.

The fix

Convert your menu to a proper webpage with sections for each course or category. On Wix/Squarespace, this is a page with text blocks. On a custom site, it's a dedicated menu page with structured HTML. Keep the PDF as a download option if you want, but make the webpage the primary version.

05

No clear call to action — or too many

Every page on your website should have exactly one primary thing you want the visitor to do. Call you. Book a table. Request a quote. Fill in a form. When a page has no CTA, visitors don't know what to do next and they leave. When a page has five CTAs — Call Us, Book Now, Download Our Brochure, Follow Us on Instagram, Sign Up for News — visitors get overwhelmed and do nothing.

Australian consumers want to be told what to do. A clear, confident button — "Get a Free Quote" or "Book a Table" — converts dramatically better than a page that assumes the visitor will figure it out.

How to check

Open your homepage. What is the ONE action you most want a visitor to take? Is there a visible button for exactly that? Is it above the fold on mobile?

The fix

Pick one primary CTA for each page. Put it in the hero (visible without scrolling). Make it a button, not a text link. Use action language: "Get a Free Quote", "Book Your Table", "Call Now" — not "Contact Us" or "Learn More". Secondary CTAs (like "Book a Call" alongside "Get a Quote") are fine, but keep the primary one visually dominant.

06

Using stock photos instead of real images

Stock photos signal "generic" before a word is read. An electrician's website with a stock photo of a man holding a hard hat doesn't show a single thing about that electrician. A real photo of the actual team, the actual van, or the actual work builds trust in a way stock photography fundamentally cannot.

Customers looking at a hospitality venue want to see the actual dining room, the actual dishes. Buyers evaluating a tradie want to see the actual finished work. Stock images not only fail to build trust — they actively signal inauthenticity to the majority of modern consumers who've been trained to spot them.

How to check

Right-click your homepage hero image and do a reverse image search. If it shows up on 50 other websites — it's stock. Replace it.

The fix

Spend one afternoon taking photos with a modern smartphone. For most small businesses this is enough. Good lighting (near a window, outdoors) and a clean background go a long way. Focus on: a headshot or team photo, 3–5 photos of your work or product, and one of your shopfront or premises. If budget allows, a 2-hour session with a local photographer typically costs $300–$600 AUD and transforms a website.

07

You've never checked Google Search Console

Google Search Console (GSC) is free. It shows you exactly which searches your site appears in, how many people click through, and whether Google has any issues indexing your pages. The vast majority of Australian small business owners have never looked at it.

Without GSC, you're flying blind. You have no idea if Google is finding your pages, which keywords are actually driving traffic, or whether a technical error is preventing your site from being indexed. GSC is the single most valuable free tool for any small business that wants to grow organic traffic.

How to check

Go to search.google.com/search-console. Have you set this up? If you haven't — stop reading and do it now. It takes 10 minutes.

The fix

Set up GSC at search.google.com/search-console. Verify your property via your domain registrar (easiest) or by adding an HTML tag. Then submit your sitemap (Settings → Sitemaps → enter your sitemap URL). Once it's collecting data (takes 2–4 weeks for meaningful data), check it monthly. Look at: which queries bring impressions, which pages are indexed, and whether there are any coverage errors.

Where to start

Check your mobile speed score first. It's the highest-impact issue and the quickest to diagnose. If you're below 70, everything else is secondary — a slow site undermines every other improvement you make.

Then work down the list. Mistakes 2, 3, and 5 can each be fixed in an afternoon on most platforms. Mistake 7 (GSC) should be set up regardless of anything else — you need the data.

If your site scores under 60 on mobile and you're on Wix or WordPress, the honest answer is that the platform is part of the problem.
