Wix vs Custom Website:
Which Is Actually Better?

The honest answer? It depends on your business — but not in the way most web designers will tell you. This guide breaks down the real cost, SEO performance, and long-term trade-offs of Wix (and Squarespace) vs a custom-built website.

By Aman Singh, Build First Site · Last updated July 2026

The Short Answer

Use Wix if...

  • • You're testing an idea with no confirmed customers yet
  • • Your site is purely informational — no SEO goals
  • • You genuinely enjoy building and maintaining it yourself
  • • You have under $800 to spend on anything digital right now

Use a custom website if...

  • • You want to rank on Google and get organic traffic
  • • You're running a business that depends on your website
  • • You want to own your code — not rent a platform
  • • You've been on Wix for 6+ months and it hasn't grown

SEO: where Wix really falls behind

Page speed is a confirmed Google ranking factor. Here's how the platforms compare on real speed tests.

Wix Squarespace Custom (Next.js)
Mobile speed score 40–65 55–70 90–100
JavaScript rendering Client-side only — Google struggles Mixed Server-side rendered — Google loves it
Schema markup Limited, no custom JSON-LD Limited Full custom schema — FAQPage, LocalBusiness, Article etc.
Sitemap control Auto-generated, limited control Auto-generated Full control
URL structure Often messy — hard to customise Decent Fully controlled

Google confirmed in 2021 that page speed is a ranking factor (Core Web Vitals). A site scoring 40 on mobile is penalised in rankings compared to a site scoring 95. Wix has improved over the years, but they're still significantly behind well-built custom sites.

When Wix actually makes sense

I'm not going to pretend Wix is always the wrong choice. Here's when it genuinely works.

You're validating a business idea

If you haven't confirmed anyone will pay you yet, spend $0 on Wix's free tier and test your idea. Don't invest in a custom site until you have customers. Once you do — upgrade.

You need something live in 48 hours

Custom websites take 2–6 weeks. If you need something before an event or media appearance and you're comfortable maintaining it — Wix is fine short-term. Plan the migration to custom within 3–6 months.

You're a hobbyist or personal project

For personal portfolios, hobby blogs, and non-commercial projects where SEO doesn't matter much, Wix or Squarespace are totally fine. Custom websites are built for businesses that depend on their site to get clients.

The real question to ask yourself

Not "Wix or custom?" — but this: what is my website supposed to do for my business?

If the answer is "rank on Google and get me customers" — a custom website will do this significantly better than Wix. If the answer is "I just need something so people can look me up" — Wix is fine.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Wix good enough for a small business website?

For a new business testing an idea? Possibly. For an established business that wants Google traffic and customers from their website? No — Wix's slow speed and limited SEO control will hold you back. A custom website built with Next.js will outrank a Wix site on the same keywords almost every time.

Is Squarespace better than Wix for SEO?

Squarespace has slightly better mobile speed scores than Wix, but both trail behind well-built custom websites significantly. Neither gives you full control over schema markup, URL structure, or server-side rendering — all of which matter for ranking.

Can I migrate my Wix website to a custom website?

Yes, but Wix doesn't export content — you'll need to rebuild the pages. We handle the migration: you give us your Wix site URL, we recreate the content in a custom Next.js build, improve the structure and SEO, and redirect your domain. Most migrations take 2–3 weeks from start to launch.

What's the difference between Wix and WordPress?

Wix is a closed platform (you rent it). WordPress is open-source software you self-host, which gives more flexibility but requires security updates, plugin management, and more technical maintenance. A custom-built Next.js website sidesteps all of this — nothing to update, nothing to hack, deployed on Vercel's global edge network.
