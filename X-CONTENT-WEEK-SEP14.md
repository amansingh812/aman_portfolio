### Post 2 — 12:00pm (EDUCATION — THREAD)
🧵 5 things I check before quoting ANY website project (1/6)

Most agencies skip these and you find out later, expensively.

2/6 — What's your actual conversion goal? "Look professional" isn't one. "Get 10 quote requests a week" is. Design follows the goal, not the other way round.

3/6 — Who's editing this after launch? If the answer is "nobody," you don't need a CMS. If it's "me, monthly," you need one that doesn't require a developer for a text change.

4/6 — What's your current site's Core Web Vitals score? Free to check, takes 30 seconds, and tells you if slow load speed is already costing you rankings.

5/6 — Do you have a domain and hosting, or does an agency "own" them for you? This one matters more than people think — I've seen businesses locked out of their own domain.

6/6 — What's the real page count? Not "a few," an actual number. It's the single biggest driver of price — see the full checklist: buildfirstsite.com/small-business-website-checklist/
Hashtags: #webdesign #smallbusinesstips #seo

### Post 2 — 12:00pm (EDUCATION — THREAD)
🧵 How much does a website actually cost in Australia? (1/5)

I get asked this weekly, so here's the honest breakdown.

2/5 — Small agencies/freelancers: roughly $800–$2,000 for a straightforward small-business site (3–10 pages). That's the range I quote too — $800 for 3–5 pages, $1,900 for up to 10.

3/5 — Bigger studios/established agencies: $3,000–$10,000+, often for the same scope, priced for their overhead rather than the build.

4/5 — Ongoing costs people forget to ask about: domain (~$20/yr), hosting, and who fixes it when something breaks. A one-off build with nobody maintaining it degrades — plugins go stale, speed drops, nothing gets updated.

5/5 — Full breakdown with real numbers, no lead-gen wall: buildfirstsite.com/how-much-does-a-website-cost-australia/
